02 · CONVERGENCE

Normalization makes text predictable

A person knows "Dog," "dog!," and "DOGS" are related. A computer doesn't — until normalization lowercases, strips punctuation, and trims whitespace so different spellings collapse to one form.

Dog → dog
dog! → dog
DOGS → dog·s
three different spellings, one normalized form — this is why matching gets easier after preprocessing
IN PRACTICE

Both steps, standard library only

import string

text = "The quick brown fox, jumps over the lazy dog!"

# 1. lowercase
text = text.lower()

# 2. remove punctuation
text = text.translate(str.maketrans('', '', string.punctuation))

# 3. split into tokens
tokens = text.split()

# ['the', 'quick', 'brown', 'fox', 'jumps', 'over', 'the', 'lazy', 'dog']
⚠ · TRADEOFFS

Preprocessing isn't free

Every normalization choice can erase information you actually needed. Toggle the example below.

✗
Languages without spaces
Chinese, Japanese, and Thai don't separate words with spaces — splitting on whitespace just doesn't work.
✗
Meaningful capitalization
German "Gift" (poison) and "gift" (English word) aren't the same — lowercasing everything can erase the distinction.
✗
Emojis & special characters
On social media, these often carry the sentiment. Deleting them can delete the actual signal.
Punctuation removal is "safe" by default — until it changes who gets eaten.
