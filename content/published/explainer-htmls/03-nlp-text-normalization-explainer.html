01 · REMOVAL

Stopwords are noise, not signal

Words like "the," "is," "in," "of" show up in almost every sentence regardless of topic. They help sentences flow, but carry no distinguishing meaning — so most pipelines strip them before anything else happens.

03 · LEMMATIZATION

Lemmatization looks it up

A lemmatizer uses vocabulary and grammar to find a word's real dictionary form — its lemma — sometimes weighing part of speech. Slower than stemming, but every output is a word that actually exists.

OriginalLemma
am, are, is→be

COMPARISON

Which one, when?

You rarely need all three. Pick the combination that matches your task.

Stemming
  • Fast, rule-based trimming
  • No grammar or context awareness
  • Good for search & fast lookup
  • Output may not be a real word
Lemmatization
  • Dictionary + grammar aware
  • Slower, more computation
  • Good for user-facing results
  • Output is always a real word
IN PRACTICE

Same sentence, three passes

import nltk
from nltk.corpus import stopwords
from nltk.stem import PorterStemmer, WordNetLemmatizer
from nltk.tokenize import word_tokenize

sentence = "The striped bats are hanging on their feet for best"
words = word_tokenize(sentence)

# 1. drop stopwords
stop_words = set(stopwords.words('english'))
filtered = [w for w in words if w.lower() not in stop_words]

# 2. stem what's left
stemmed = [PorterStemmer().stem(w) for w in filtered]

# 3. lemmatize what's left
lemmatized = [WordNetLemmatizer().lemmatize(w) for w in filtered]
